# PPC Study Plan

## 1. Draw and label the three points
- On the curve — resources fully and efficiently employed.
- Inside the curve — resources unemployed or used inefficiently.
- Outside the curve — unattainable with current resources and technology.

Draw this from memory until it's automatic.

## 2. Explain why it's concave
Factors of production aren't perfectly substitutable between uses. Fertile land grows wheat well but is poor for mining, and vice versa. As you push more resources into one good, you're forced to use increasingly unsuitable resources for it, so the opportunity cost of each extra unit rises. This is the law of increasing opportunity cost. Say this in your own words, not just the label.

## 3. Calculate opportunity cost from PPC numbers
Practice both types.
- A straight-line PPC has constant opportunity cost, same ratio no matter where you are on it.
- A concave PPC has increasing opportunity cost, the ratio worsens as you move along it.

Given an equation or a data table, find intercepts, read off values, calculate what's given up when output shifts. Redo the 4A + 5M = 200 example from your workbook without looking at the answer first.

## 4. Know outward shift causes versus inward shift causes
- Outward shift, economic growth — more resources (population growth, new land, capital investment) or better technology.
- Inward shift, negative growth — natural disaster, war, resource depletion, workforce emigration.

Generate your own example for each, not just the category label.

## 5. Nail the three-way trap
- A movement **along** the curve reallocates existing resources between two goods and has an opportunity cost.
- A **shift** of the curve changes total productive capacity, that's growth or decline.
- A point moving from **inside** the curve **to** the curve is neither, it's better use of existing resources, like falling unemployment, and it is NOT economic growth.

Mix these three up on a test and you lose easy marks.
