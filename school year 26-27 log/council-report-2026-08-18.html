The Contrarian

Six affirmation-goals recited every morning and not one has a this-week action attached. That's a wish list dressed up as discipline. Harvard, all A*, 1500 SAT, top 50 WGHSIC, Diamond Challenge, RSI all compete for the same hours — nobody ranks them, so the ranking happens by accident.

The bigger flag is the knee. "Still kinda iffy" next to "made it through" flag football is playing through uncertainty, not a managed return. No physio, no doctor, no rehab plan mentioned — "gradually build it back in" is a hope, not a protocol.

Then three-plus hours of serious engineering work the same night, after a full first day back. That's an aggressive baseline for day one of fifty-plus more days like it. If this is the norm and not the exception, it shows up as a crash in October — either the coding stops or the grades stop, not gracefully.

The First Principles Thinker

Strip the six affirmations down: what is day 1 actually optimizing for? Most of that list is an outcome, not a lever — Harvard, the SAT score, WGHSIC placing, all downstream of hundreds of small decisions and things nobody controls. Reciting outcomes is motivating for three weeks, then it's background noise.

The real question: pick the one or two goals with a deadline this term and build a weekly cadence around those. That's the input/output split — daily attention goes on hours of SAT prep, hours on the app, knee rehab consistency, not on the acceptance letter itself.

Worth naming: the hotel app work isn't a distraction from the goals, it's evidence for them. A shipped feature with tests is a stronger Harvard data point than the affirmation about Harvard. The log treats them as separate buckets. They're not.

But look at what shipped on literally the first day back: five real changes to a production-shaped Django app — a documented call not to delete a fragile monkeypatch, a bug fix traced to a specific failure mode, a dead table dropped, an unused model removed cleanly, and a full feature built with seventeen tests and a second bug caught in review. Second upside: Mr Alun teaches econ and reads as worth a real relationship — strict enough to be useful, personable enough to last. Given the real estate/finance direction, that's a resource to use deliberately this year.
