ATHLETIC PERFORMANCE PROGRAM v3.1 - BUILD PHASE Sport-free training year (returning to goalkeeper / basketball guard / karting ~July 2027) Built around: general strength, hypertrophy, and a maintained aerobic and pull-up base, with a small block of knee and ankle capacity work added after the council review of 2 September 2026, and a push/pull volume rebalance added the same week.

Before you start: sets and loads are built for pain-free training. Fatigue and normal muscle burn are expected. Sharp, sudden, or joint-line pain is not. If that happens on any exercise, stop that movement for the day and check in with a doctor or physio before continuing that lift.

FRAMING CORRECTION (read this once)

The goal is no longer written as realignment or valgus prevention. Structural alignment, femoral anteversion, tibial torsion, Q angle, does not change with training. What changes is strength, tendon stiffness, range of motion under load, and motor control.

Prevention programmes reduce knee injury in athletes because they run in-season, alongside the exposure. You have no exposure until July 2027, so there is nothing to prevent this year. This is a capacity year. Landing, reactive agility and cutting work belongs in the April to July 2027 conversion block, not now.

That means the additions below are chosen to fill genuine gaps in load tolerance, not to correct anything.

WHAT CHANGED FROM v2, AND WHY

Added, five items, roughly 11 minutes total across the week:

1. Loaded ankle rocker, Tuesday and Thursday warm-ups. Ankle dorsiflexion range is one of the few variables with a repeatable mechanical link to knee position in squatting and landing, and it is cheap to train and easy to measure.
2. Seated or bent-knee calf raise, Thursday. Gastrocnemius crosses the knee and only loads properly with the knee straight. Soleus does not cross the knee and takes most of the load when the knee is bent. v2 trained one and not the other.
3. Reverse Nordic or assisted sissy squat, Tuesday. Nothing in v2 loaded the quadriceps at long muscle length with the hip extended.
4. Eccentric step-down, Thursday. Single-leg eccentric control through deep knee flexion, which the Bulgarian split squat and single-leg RDL do not cover.
5. Copenhagen adduction, Friday. v2 had no adductor work at all after the hip machine was cut. Holds first, then progress the lever.

Replaced: the bent-knee calf isometric hold, 3x30 sec each leg, is gone as a standing item. A loaded seated raise through full range is a stronger stimulus than an unloaded hold. One heavy 30 second bent-knee hold survives at the end of the calf block as the Berlin Protocol substitute.

Reduced to pay for the time: single-leg Romanian deadlift drops from 3 sets to 2.

Kept, with the reasoning now settled: countermovement jump and lateral bound stay at current volume. v2 flagged an inconsistency about whether they belonged in a no-sport year. Resolved as a dose question rather than a yes or no. Low-dose jumping builds tendon stiffness cheaply and stops the conversion block starting from zero. High-volume reactive agility still waits until 2027.

Declined, so you do not have to re-litigate them: Poliquin step-up, weighted curtsy lunge, the Berlin Protocol as written, weighted wall sits, sit-to-stand, single-leg bear squats, knee pulses, TFL-biased hip abduction, side planks. Reasons are in the DECLINED section at the end.

Everything else from v2 is unchanged.

WHAT CHANGED FROM v3 TO v3.1, AND WHY

This round came from a straight volume audit, sets per muscle per week rather than exercise count. v3 undercounted two things. Side delts sat at zero. Horizontal rowing sat at 3 sets against 9 to 11 for vertical pulling.

Added: Lateral Raise, Monday, 3x12-15. Nothing else in the week trains the side delt, and it is the head that makes shoulders look wide.

Added: Pec Deck or mid-height Cable Fly, Monday, 3x12. Bench and incline both press in a similar range. A fly loads the pec at long muscle length, which has a better hypertrophy return than short-range work alone.

Increased: Incline Bench Press, Monday, 3x8 to 4x8. Chest goes from 7 sets to 11.

Added: Chest-Supported Row or Single-Arm Dumbbell Row, Wednesday, 3x10.

Increased: Seated Row Machine, Wednesday, 3x10 to 4x10. Horizontal rowing goes from 3 sets to 7.

Added: Hanging Leg Raise, Friday, 3x8-12, straight after the dead hang. The bar is already in your hand at that point in the session.

Settled: the Overhead Press stays standing, barbell. A Norwegian comparison found standing produces more deltoid activation and seated allows slightly more load, so the two roughly cancel. Switch rule instead of a fixed answer. Run a set of 6 standing and watch what fails first. Shoulders give out, stay standing. Lower back arches, or you start using your trunk to help the bar up instead of your shoulders, move to seated barbell. Your trunk should never be the limiter on a shoulder lift.

Settled: the Cable Kneeling Crunch gets a form fix rather than a swap. You are almost certainly hinging at the hips and pulling with your lats instead of flexing your spine. Lock the hip angle, keep your elbows fixed relative to your head so your arms act as a hook and not a lever, round your back, pull your ribcage toward your pelvis, go lighter than feels right, pause one second at the bottom. Give it two sessions with that form. If you still feel nothing after two sessions, drop it. The Friday hanging leg raise covers trunk flexion on its own.

Declined: the cross-body cable pull, cable at shoulder height, pulled across the face. That path is horizontal adduction, the arm swinging across the front of the body, not abduction, the arm lifting away from the body to the side. Horizontal adduction loads front delt and pec, both already covered by your pressing work. It does not put meaningful tension on the middle delt, which only loads when the arm moves out to the side in the frontal plane. The dumbbell lateral raise added above is the actual fix for the side delt gap. Adding the cross-body version on top would be redundant volume on a muscle group that is no longer short on it.

Time added: roughly 6 minutes Monday, 7 minutes Wednesday, 4 minutes Friday. Thursday is untouched and stays the longest day in the week.

PROGRESSION

Main lifts: add 2.5kg to squat, deadlift, RDL, bench, and overhead press once you complete all prescribed reps with 1-2 reps left in reserve.

Machine, dumbbell and band-assisted work: add load or reduce band assistance once the top of the rep range feels controlled for two sessions in a row. This covers the Pec Fly, Lateral Raise, and Chest-Supported or Single-Arm Row added in v3.1.

Hanging Leg Raise: progress by reps within the 8-12 range first, then add ankle weight once 3x12 feels controlled for two sessions in a row.

Deadlift: same 2.5kg scheme, no pause. Watch that your hips and shoulders rise together as the bar leaves the floor. If your hips shoot up first and your shoulders stay low, or a rep feels like your lower back is doing more work than your legs, stop adding weight that session.

Copenhagen adduction, eight-week progression. Weeks 1-2, short-lever hold, bottom knee on the floor, top leg supported at the knee on the bench, hold 15-20 sec each side. Weeks 3-4, short-lever reps, 6-8 each side. Weeks 5-6, long-lever hold, support at the ankle, 15-20 sec each side. Weeks 7-8, long-lever reps, 6-8 each side. This exercise produces severe soreness if you start too high, which is why it starts with holds. Do not skip stages because a stage felt easy on the day.

Reverse Nordic: progress by range first, then by tempo, then by load. Add a slow 3 second lower before you add a plate.

Eccentric step-down: progress by step height before load, then hold a dumbbell.

Loaded ankle rocker: progress by adding load on the thigh, and track the knee-to-wall measurement rather than how it feels.

Calf isometric: progress by load, hold time stays at 30 sec.

Intervals: hold the same work rate as the weeks go on rather than increasing distance.

BASELINES AND TRACKING

Record these in week one, before any of the new work starts. Without them you cannot tell in March whether any of this worked.

1. Knee-to-wall dorsiflexion, in centimetres, each side. Foot pointing at the wall, heel down, drive the knee to touch the wall, measure the toe-to-wall distance at the furthest point you can still touch. Retest monthly.
2. Single-leg calf raise reps to failure, each side, knee straight. Retest every 8 weeks.
3. A phone video of a single-leg squat, each leg, filmed from the front. Retest every 8 weeks. This is the only record you will have of how the knee actually moves.
4. Seven consecutive morning bodyweights to replace the provisional 72.5kg.

Ongoing: weigh in every morning, same conditions, straight after waking. Ignore the daily number, track the weekly average.

Bodyweight is the item most likely to be neglected and it matters more than any exercise on this page. You squat 80kg at roughly 72.5kg. Over ten months, getting stronger and heavier does more for knee robustness than the entire accessory list.

DECLINED, AND WHY

Poliquin step-up. The premise is selective VMO strengthening. A systematic review of EMG studies found that altering hip, knee, ankle or foot position does not preferentially activate VMO over vastus lateralis. It trains the quad, which the squat already does under more load.

Weighted curtsy lunge. Loads the knee in adduction and internal rotation, the position the goal argues against, and you already report no connection with it.

Berlin Protocol as written. The research is real, but the protocol requires 90% of maximum voluntary contraction, verified in the original studies on a dynamometer and in the clinical trial through a force-gauge sling with live feedback. Without force measurement the load is a guess. Replaced with the single heavy 30 second bent-knee isometric on Thursday.

Weighted wall sits, sit-to-stand, single-leg bear squats, knee pulses. Redundant with the leg press, split squat and step-down.

TFL-biased hip abduction. Selective muscle biasing within a group does not hold up under EMG measurement, same problem as the VMO claim.

Side planks. The Pallof press already covers anti-rotation and anti-lateral-flexion trunk work.

Cross-body cable pull for side delts, cable at shoulder height, standing perpendicular, pulled across the face. This is horizontal adduction, the arm swinging across the front of the body, not abduction, the arm lifting away from the body to the side. It loads front delt and pec, both already covered by the pressing work. It does not train the middle delt. The dumbbell Lateral Raise added in v3.1 is the actual fix for that gap.

Mobility flows, 90/90, pigeon, seiza, squat holds. Fine as a five minute warm-up before leg days if you enjoy them. Not a training input, and not tracked.

Seated weighted hip flexion, hip flexor fallbacks, rotational power work, anti-extension work. Deferred rather than declined. These become relevant for sprint mechanics and sport-specific output in the conversion block, not in a capacity year.

Tibialis raises. Optional. Thin evidence, near-zero cost. If you want them, 2x20 between other sets on Thursday. Do not let them displace anything.

TIMELINE

Build phase: now through roughly late March 2027. Strength and size are the priority, this is the plan above.

Conversion phase: roughly April through July 2027, 10-12 weeks. Sprint volume rises, reactive agility and landing work return, jump training shifts from output to landing control under fatigue, absolute strength tapers to maintenance.

Write the conversion block entry criteria in February 2027, not in April. If the standard for readiness does not exist before the block starts, April arrives and the same guesswork repeats. The three target activities also differ, goalkeeping, basketball and karting, and karting loads the neck and trunk rather than the knee. One conversion block will not serve all three equally, so decide in February which one leads.
